Bitcoin fell below the price that many commentators had treated as the bottom of this downturn. On Saturday, November 21, 2026, it touched about $55,000, its lowest level since September 2024. It then bounced and slipped again. This post explains what happened, what the "four-year cycle" argument is, and what a fall of this size means if you own bitcoin or are thinking about it. It makes no price predictions and recommends no trades.

It is a snapshot **as of Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (UTC)**. Prices move around the clock, so by the time you read this the numbers will have changed.

## Who this is for, and who it isn't

**It's for:** anyone who owns bitcoin or a bitcoin ETF, anyone tempted to buy "because it's cheap now," and anyone who wants to read the headlines without being pushed by them.

**It isn't** investment advice. It does not say bitcoin has bottomed, or that it hasn't.

## What is confirmed

Figures differ a little between sources, because exchanges price bitcoin separately and some sites use closing prices while others use the lowest trade of the day.

- **The low.** U.Today reported on November 22 that bitcoin fell to $55,081 on November 21, its lowest since September 2024. Decrypt, citing CoinGecko, put it at $55,298, and a market summary on KuCoin's news page said $55,030. An earlier break of $60,000 came on November 20, with an intraday low of $57,716 according to a TradingView / Crypto Briefing report.
- **The level that was broken.** CoinDesk gave about $57,700 as the low from June to July, which many commentators treated as the low of this downturn. A price-data page I read (JournalArta) lists the 2026 closing-basis low as $58,559 on June 30. The two differ because of the way they measure, but either way the November move went below it.
- **From the record.** Bitcoin's record was above $126,000 in October 2025 (Decrypt gives $126,080). At about $55,000 it was more than 56% below that.
- **The month.** It opened November at $77,425, according to U.Today, and by November 22 was down about 21.6% for the month, the worst month since June 2022, citing CryptoRank.
- **Since the low.** Bitcoin rebounded to about $60,600 on Sunday and about $61,300 on Monday, November 23 (Decrypt). It reached about $64,372 on Monday evening, in a rally that coincided with Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's CEO, then fell on Tuesday and Wednesday. On Wednesday, November 25, JournalArta had it at about $58,600 and Cryptonomist about $58,900 on Binance. Both described the day's price as still moving.

## What was blamed, and what is opinion

- **Leverage.** Crypto Briefing reported about $2 billion of long positions liquidated in the 24 hours before the drop. Decrypt reported more than $2 billion over the weekend (attributed to Tim Sun of HashKey Group). A liquidation is a forced sale of a leveraged bet, and it can speed a fall up.
- **ETF outflows.** Crypto Briefing reported $582 million of net outflows from US spot bitcoin ETFs on November 19. Decrypt reported $669.2 million on Friday, November 20, the largest in a day since June 25. Cryptonomist reports about $570 million on November 25, with November's total approaching $5 billion. I could not check these against fund data, and outflows describe what investors did, not why.
- **Stocks and rates.** Several outlets tie the move to a selloff in AI-linked and technology stocks, a stronger dollar, and expectations of Federal Reserve rate rises. Decrypt, citing CME FedWatch, had the chance of a quarter-point rise on December 9 at 96.2% on November 23. That is market pricing, not a decision. See [why tech stocks and crypto are falling together](/blog/why-tech-stocks-and-crypto-are-falling-together/) and [what the Federal Reserve does and how rate decisions affect your money](/blog/what-the-federal-reserve-does-and-how-rate-decisions-affect-your-money/).
- **Opinion, not forecast.** A commentator quoted by KuCoin said a weekly close above $57,700 would make the weekend drop a one-off spike, while a close below would put the "realised price" (the average cost of all coins held) near $53,600 in play. BitMEX co-founder Arthur Hayes told Decrypt that $50,000 would be "the next real test" if the Nasdaq keeps falling. Cryptonomist notes that an oversold reading alone does not mark a bottom. These are views, and this post predicts nothing.

## The four-year cycle argument, and its limits

Some analysts point to a pattern tied to the "halving," the scheduled cut in the reward miners earn, which has happened roughly every four years (July 2016, May 2020, April 2024, with the next expected around April 2028). U.Today reports supporters of the theory saying each bear market had its final sell-off in 2014, 2018 and 2022, which would put 2026 next. It also notes that bitcoin's 2022 low of about $15,500 came on November 21, 2022, four years to the day before this one. The supporters are not named in the article.

Reasons to be careful with it:

- **Three examples.** The pattern rests on only three earlier cycles, which is very few.
- **It is not clean.** U.Today's own timeline puts the 2014 cycle's bottom in January 2015.
- **A bottom is visible only afterwards.** A matching date does not show that a low is in. If the price falls further, the theory can be stretched to fit that too.
- **The market has changed.** Today there are spot ETFs, large leveraged trading, and a price that has moved with stocks and rates, which the cycle story does not account for.
- **It is not a rule.** Nobody is obliged to repeat history.

## A worked example: why a big fall is hard to recover from

These use the record price above and are rounded.

- From about $126,080 to $55,081 is a fall of about **56.3%**.
- To get back to the record from the low, the price must rise about **128.9%**.
- From about $58,600 the price is about 53.5% below the record, and it needs about **115%** to return to it.

An invented $10,000 shows it: bought at the record, that is worth about $4,369 at the low, and it would need to more than double to get back to $10,000. A fall is measured from a higher starting point than the recovery that follows, so the climb back is steeper than the drop. The month of November makes the same point more gently: $10,000 at the November 1 price of $77,425 would be about $7,570 at about $58,600, a loss of roughly 24%.

## What this means for you

- **Know what you own and why.** If you hold bitcoin, write down the share of your savings it represents and what the money is for. See [what is cryptocurrency](/blog/what-is-cryptocurrency-how-it-works-and-the-risks/) and [Bitcoin vs Ethereum vs stablecoins](/blog/bitcoin-vs-ethereum-vs-stablecoins-whats-the-difference/).
- **Cheaper is not the same as safer.** A lower price shows what bitcoin costs today. It says nothing about where it goes next, and it has fallen over 50% already.
- **Money you need soon does not belong here.** Rent, tuition, a down payment or an emergency fund should not depend on a price that can move 20% in a month. See [what stock market volatility means for your savings](/blog/what-stock-market-volatility-means-for-your-savings/).
- **Leverage magnifies this.** The liquidations above are what happens to borrowed positions. A loss on a leveraged position can be larger than the money you put in.
- **Be careful with "buy the dip" and "it's over" messages alike.** Both claim to know what nobody knows. See [how to read crypto news without getting burned](/blog/how-to-read-crypto-news-without-getting-burned/).
- **Keep your coins safe, whatever the price.** Falling markets bring more scams. See [how to store crypto safely](/blog/how-to-store-crypto-safely-wallets-exchanges-and-common-scams/).

## Common mistakes

- **Treating a date match as a forecast.** The cycle theory is a story built on three data points.
- **Selling in a panic or buying in a panic.** Both are decisions made by a headline.
- **Averaging down without a limit.** Adding to a falling position raises your exposure to it.
- **Forgetting taxes and fees** when you trade. Rules differ by country.
- **Using one price source** and assuming the number is the only one.

## What is still unknown

- Whether $55,000 holds, and for how long.
- Whether ETF outflows slow, or continue.
- What the Federal Reserve does on December 9, and how markets react.
- Whether the link between bitcoin and tech stocks stays as tight as it has been.

## Getting started

1. **Check how much bitcoin you hold,** including through ETFs and funds.
2. **Write down what that money is for** and when you need it.
3. **Decide in advance** what you will and won't do if the price moves another 20% in either direction.
4. **Don't borrow to buy it.**
5. **Come back to the numbers** after the Fed's December meeting.
