EPF and PPF both hand you tax-free growth on money you won't touch for decades, but they aren't interchangeable — one is a mandatory, employer-linked account that comes bundled with a salaried job, and the other is a voluntary account anyone can open on their own. If you're salaried, EPF is already happening in the background whether you think about it or not. PPF is the one you have to choose to open.

## How each one actually works

**EPF (Employees' Provident Fund):** if you work for a company covered under the EPF Act, both you and your employer contribute 12% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance every month. Your full 12% goes into EPF. Of your employer's 12%, only 3.67% lands in EPF — the remaining 8.33% is diverted into the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), a separate pension pot with its own payout rules. The EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation) declares an interest rate annually; it's been holding at **8.25%** through FY 2024-25 and into the current year. Interest and withdrawals are tax-free once you've completed five years of continuous service, and your own contribution qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C, up to the usual ₹1.5 lakh annual cap.

**PPF (Public Provident Fund):** a government-backed account open to any resident Indian — employed, self-employed, or neither. You open it yourself at a bank or post office and contribute voluntarily, anywhere from ₹500 to ₹1.5 lakh a year. The interest rate is set by the Ministry of Finance every quarter rather than once a year like EPF's; it's currently **7.1%**, where it's sat for several quarters running. PPF carries full EEE tax status — contribution, interest, and maturity amount are all tax-free, no five-year clock required. The trade-off is a genuine **15-year lock-in** from the date you open it, extendable afterward in 5-year blocks.

## The rule that catches people off guard: the ₹2.5 lakh threshold

EPF's tax-free interest isn't unconditional. If your own annual contribution (EPF plus any Voluntary Provident Fund top-up) exceeds **₹2.5 lakh** in a year, interest on the amount above that gets taxed — the threshold rises to ₹5 lakh only if your employer isn't contributing at all, which is rare for anyone this applies to. High earners who voluntarily inflate their VPF contributions to chase EPF's rate can walk straight into this without realizing it. PPF has no equivalent ceiling on tax-free interest — its cap is simply the ₹1.5 lakh annual contribution limit itself.

## A worked example: same 8.25% rate, very different access

Say someone earns a ₹40,000 basic salary. Their own EPF contribution is 12% of that, or **₹4,800 a month** — ₹57,600 a year, matched by an equivalent flow from their employer (minus the EPS split above). At 8.25% compounded annually, steady contributions at this level grow to roughly **₹96 lakh over 30 years** — a number that beats most fixed deposits and debt funds on a pre-tax-equivalent basis, since the interest itself is untaxed after five years' service.

Now say a self-employed consultant with no EPF access puts the PPF maximum, ₹1.5 lakh, into their account every year instead. At 7.1%, that grows to roughly **₹40.7 lakh after 15 years** — the account's full lock-in period, not 30 years. The rate is lower, but so is the horizon: PPF was never meant to run three decades in one uninterrupted stretch the way an EPF account typically does across a career.

## Who this is for — and who it isn't

**It's for:** anyone deciding whether they need to open a PPF account on top of the EPF they may already have running through payroll, or weighing PPF as their main retirement vehicle if they're self-employed or unemployed.

**It's not for:** people trying to work out NPS (National Pension System) in detail — it's a third, market-linked option that often sits alongside EPF and PPF rather than competing directly with either, and isn't covered here. ## What to actually do

- **Salaried with EPF running automatically:** don't ignore it just because it's invisible. Check your EPF passbook via the EPFO portal periodically, and know that job changes require an active transfer, not a fresh account — leaving an old EPF account dormant means it stops earning interest after three years of inactivity.
- **Self-employed, freelance, or no employer contribution:** PPF is your main tax-advantaged, government-backed option. Opening it as early as possible matters more than the amount in year one, since the 15-year clock starts from account opening, not from when you start contributing seriously.
- **Salaried with room to save beyond EPF:** a PPF account isn't redundant just because EPF exists — PPF's 15-year lock-in with no employment dependency makes it useful as a separate goal-specific account (a child's education, a fixed date 15 years out) rather than a general EPF duplicate.
- **High earner near the ₹2.5 lakh EPF contribution threshold:** run the numbers on VPF top-ups before assuming the full 8.25% rate applies tax-free to every rupee — above that line, PPF's uncapped tax-free interest can actually come out ahead net of tax.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming PPF interest is comparable to EPF's without accounting for access.** EPF and PPF interest look close (8.25% vs. 7.1%), but EPF is normally an uninterrupted multi-decade compounding account tied to continuous employment, while PPF locks each contribution to a fixed 15-year account life — they aren't really competing for the same money.
- **Not realizing partial PPF withdrawal only starts in year 7.** Before the 7th financial year, PPF money is genuinely inaccessible except through a loan against the balance (available from year 3 to year 6, capped at 25% of the balance from two years prior). Treat PPF contributions as locked, not as a flexible backup.
- **Letting an old EPF account go dormant.** An EPF account with no contributions for 36 straight months stops earning interest — always transfer, never abandon, when changing jobs.
- **Overlooking the EPS split.** Only 3.67% of your employer's 12% contribution actually reaches your EPF balance; the other 8.33% goes to EPS, a separate pension scheme with its own eligibility and payout rules — it doesn't compound inside your EPF corpus the way the headline "12% + 12%" framing implies.

## Getting started

If you're salaried, log into the EPFO member portal (or ask your employer's HR/payroll team) to check your current EPF balance and confirm past employers' accounts have been properly transferred rather than left dormant. To open a PPF account, any nationalized bank, most private banks, or a post office can set one up in a single visit with basic KYC documents. The figures above (8.25% EPF, 7.1% PPF, ₹2.5 lakh EPF tax-free interest threshold, ₹1.5 lakh PPF annual cap) are current as of this writing — EPF's rate is reset annually by the EPFO and PPF's every quarter by the Ministry of Finance, so check epfindia.gov.in or your bank/post office for the live rate before making contribution decisions.
