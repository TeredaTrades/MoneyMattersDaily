Debt payoff apps split into two real categories, and mixing them up is the most common reason people pick the wrong one. Some apps are dedicated payoff planners — you enter your balances and interest rates, pick a strategy, and the app shows your debt-free date. Others are budgeting or spending apps that treat debt payoff as one feature among many. Neither is "better" in the abstract; the right pick depends on whether your problem is not having a plan, or not knowing where your money goes each month. Pricing and features below are approximate and change; check each app's current site before signing up.

## Undebt.it — best free option if you already have a plan

Undebt.it is a web-based planner, not a bank-connected app — you enter each debt's balance, rate, and minimum manually, and it builds a month-by-month schedule.

- **Cost:** Free for the core planner; a paid tier adds extras like PDF exports.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who already knows their numbers and just wants a clear payoff timeline across snowball, avalanche, or a custom order, without linking a bank account.
- **Downside:** Nothing updates automatically. If a balance changes, you update it yourself — that's the tradeoff for not connecting your accounts.

## YNAB — best if the real problem is your budget, not your debt tracker

YNAB isn't a debt app first — it's a zero-based budgeting app with a loan payoff planner built in, so it treats debt as something you fix by finding money in your budget to throw at it.

- **Cost:** $14.99/month or roughly $99/year, with a free trial.
- **Good fit for:** Someone whose debt keeps growing because they don't know where their paycheck goes each month — the budgeting habit is the actual fix here, not just a payoff calculator.
- **Downside:** It's the most expensive app on this list and has a real learning curve. If you already budget fine and just want a payoff plan, you're paying for a system you don't need.

## Debt Payoff Planner — best dedicated mobile tracker

This app does one thing: shows you an exact debt-free date based on your balances, rates, and payments, with snowball, avalanche, or custom strategies and progress charts to keep you motivated.

- **Cost:** Free with a 2-debt limit; premium is roughly $6/month or $25/year for unlimited debts and device syncing.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who wants a no-nonsense mobile app focused entirely on the debt-free countdown, without budgeting features they won't use.
- **Downside:** No bank sync or automatic payments — you're tracking manually, and the free tier's 2-debt cap means most people with more than a couple of balances will need to upgrade.

## Rocket Money — best first step if overspending is the actual leak

Rocket Money isn't really a debt payoff app — it finds forgotten subscriptions and overpriced bills, negotiates some of them down, and helps you cancel what you don't use, freeing up cash you can redirect toward debt.

- **Cost:** $6–$12/month (you choose within that range); bill negotiation takes a separate percentage of whatever it saves you.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who suspects recurring charges are quietly draining money that could be going to debt, and wants that fixed before picking a payoff strategy.
- **Downside:** It doesn't build a payoff plan at all. Pair it with a dedicated tracker like Undebt.it or Debt Payoff Planner once you've freed up the extra cash.

## Empower — best free all-in-one if you don't want another app

Empower bundles a debt payoff tool into its broader free dashboard, alongside net worth tracking and budgeting, so you see debt in the context of your full financial picture rather than in isolation.

- **Cost:** Free for the budgeting and debt tools; Empower also offers a paid wealth-management service, but you don't need it to use the debt payoff feature.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who wants debt, net worth, and spending in one free dashboard instead of paying for a single-purpose app.
- **Downside:** The debt payoff planner is a smaller piece of a much bigger app, so it's less deep than a dedicated tracker — and a free app built around a paid upsell will periodically remind you the upsell exists.

## How to actually choose

Start with what's actually broken: **no plan, no budget, or a spending leak.** If you know your numbers and just need a schedule, Undebt.it costs nothing. If debt keeps coming back because you don't know where money goes, YNAB fixes the upstream problem, not just the symptom. If you want a mobile app built around one countdown, Debt Payoff Planner does that and nothing else. If you suspect subscriptions are the leak, start with Rocket Money and pair it with a tracker after. And if you'd rather see debt alongside your whole financial picture for free, Empower does that without a subscription.

One thing to watch across all of them: none of these apps pay down debt for you. Automatic payments, reminders, and payoff dates are motivation tools — the actual progress still comes from the payments you make, on time, every month.
