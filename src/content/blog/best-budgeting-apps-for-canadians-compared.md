A budgeting app that works beautifully in the US can fall apart the moment you try to connect it to RBC, TD, or a local credit union — broken bank feeds, no support for CAD-native accounts, and zero awareness that TFSAs and RRSPs exist. The five apps below were all picked specifically for how they handle being used from Canada, not just imported from a US-first list. Pricing is shown in CAD where the provider publishes it that way, and in USD (with an approximate CAD conversion) where it doesn't — check each app's own pricing page before signing up, since both the price and the exchange rate move.

## YNAB — best zero-based methodology, priced in USD

YNAB's four-rule, zero-based system (give every dollar a job before you spend it) is the same regardless of what country you're in, and it does sync with the major Canadian banks.

- **Cost:** $14.99 USD/month or $109 USD/year (no CAD pricing — that works out to roughly $20-21 CAD/month depending on the exchange rate), after a 34-day free trial.
- **Good fit for:** Someone sold on the zero-based method itself who doesn't mind paying in USD and manually noting which categories are RRSP or TFSA contributions, since YNAB has no built-in concept of either.
- **Downside:** No native TFSA/RRSP tracking, and USD-only pricing means your actual cost creeps up whenever the Canadian dollar weakens.

## KOHO — best if you want budgeting built into your spending account

KOHO is a Canadian-built prepaid Mastercard with real budgeting features layered on top — spending categories, round-up savings, and credit-building — rather than a budgeting app that connects to accounts you already have.

- **Cost:** Free for the base account; paid tiers run up to roughly $12-14 CAD/month for extras like a higher cashback rate and a bigger interest-free cash advance.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who's fine routing everyday spending through a new prepaid card in exchange for built-in budgeting, round-up savings, and credit building in one place.
- **Downside:** KOHO only tracks money that moves through the KOHO card itself — it doesn't link to your existing RBC or TD chequing account, so it isn't a real fit if you want one app to see everything you already have.

## PocketSmith — best for cash-flow forecasting, with a caveat

PocketSmith's calendar-based forecasting shows your projected balance weeks or months out, not just what already happened, which is unusual among budgeting apps.

- **Cost:** Free tier covers bank feeds for two accounts; Foundation is $9.99 USD/month billed annually ($14.95 month-to-month), with pricier tiers above that.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who wants to see whether a bill three weeks out will overdraw an account, not just review last month's spending.
- **Downside:** PocketSmith is New Zealand-built, prices in USD, and states on its own site that Canadian bank feeds (run through Yodlee and Plaid) have ongoing issues with some institutions — worth confirming your specific bank connects cleanly before paying for a plan.

## Wealthica — best for seeing TFSA/RRSP accounts alongside spending

Wealthica is a Canadian aggregator built to pull together bank accounts, brokerages, and registered accounts — including TFSAs and RRSPs — into one net-worth view, which is exactly the piece the other apps on this list mostly skip.

- **Cost:** Free tier available; paid plans start around $5+ CAD/month for deeper features.
- **Good fit for:** Someone whose real gap isn't day-to-day spending tracking but seeing registered accounts, brokerages, and banking side by side in one Canadian-built dashboard.
- **Downside:** It's built around net worth and account aggregation more than category-by-category budgeting, so it's a poor fit if a monthly spending plan is what you actually want.

## Wealthsimple — free, but only if you're already a customer

Wealthsimple bundles a basic spending/budgeting view into its existing banking and investing app, in CAD, with no separate signup.

- **Cost:** Free, but only as part of a Wealthsimple Cash or investing account — there's no standalone budgeting product.
- **Good fit for:** An existing Wealthsimple customer who wants a no-cost, no-extra-app way to see spending alongside the accounts they already hold there.
- **Downside:** It's not a serious option if you're not already banking or investing with Wealthsimple, and the budgeting features are noticeably thinner than a dedicated app like YNAB or PocketSmith.

## How to actually choose

Start with the thing you actually can't do without: **real Canadian bank sync, TFSA/RRSP visibility, or a genuinely free tier.** If you want a proven, hands-on methodology and don't mind paying in USD, YNAB is still the most-tested option. If you'd rather have budgeting built into an account you're already spending from, KOHO does that natively. If forecasting a few weeks ahead matters more than looking backward, PocketSmith is built for exactly that — just confirm your bank's feed works before paying. If registered accounts are the piece you're missing, Wealthica is the Canadian-built option built around exactly that gap. And if you already bank with Wealthsimple, its built-in view costs nothing extra to turn on.

One Canada-specific trap worth naming: a lot of "best budgeting apps" content online is written for a US audience first and Canada second, which means TFSA and RRSP contributions often get lumped in as generic "savings" or "investing" transactions with no distinction from a regular chequing withdrawal. If keeping registered-account contributions visible as their own category matters to you, check for that specifically — most apps built outside Canada won't have thought about it.
