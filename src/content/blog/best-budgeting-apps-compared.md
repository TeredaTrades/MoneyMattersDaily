Mint shut down in 2024, which pushed a lot of people into this decision at once. There's no single "best" app here — the right one depends on whether you want to actively plan every dollar or just want a clean picture of where your money already went. Pricing below is approximate and changes; check each app's current pricing page before signing up.

## YNAB (You Need a Budget) — best if you want to change your habits

YNAB is a zero-based budgeting app: every dollar you have gets assigned a job before you spend it, not after. It's the most hands-on option on this list, and that's the whole point — it's built for people who want the app to change how they think about spending, not just track it.

- **Cost:** Around $109/year, no permanent free tier (it offers a trial).
- **Good fit for:** People who've tried "just tracking" apps and found that watching the numbers after the fact didn't change their behavior.
- **Downside:** Real learning curve. If you want something that runs quietly in the background, this isn't it — it expects regular engagement.

## Monarch Money — best for couples and households

Monarch grew largely as the app former Mint users migrated to. It's built around shared visibility — both partners see the same dashboard, same categories, same goals — plus net worth tracking across all linked accounts.

- **Cost:** Roughly $100/year.
- **Good fit for:** Couples or households managing money jointly, and anyone who wants full account aggregation (checking, credit cards, investments, loans) in one place.
- **Downside:** Requires linking your bank logins, which not everyone is comfortable with. The interface takes a bit longer to learn than some competitors.

## Copilot Money — best design, but Apple-only

Copilot is consistently the pick for people who care about visual design and are already in the Apple ecosystem — it's iOS/Mac only, no Android or web version.

- **Cost:** Roughly $100/year.
- **Good fit for:** iPhone users who want a polished, fast, well-categorized view of spending and investments without much manual setup.
- **Downside:** If anyone in your household is on Android, this is off the table for shared use.

## Rocket Money — best free tier, narrowest scope

Rocket Money's core value isn't budgeting — it's finding and canceling subscriptions you forgot about, and negotiating bills on your behalf for a cut of the savings. The free tier does real work: subscription detection, basic tracking.

- **Cost:** Free tier available; Premium adds bill negotiation and more features, priced on a pay-what-you-want-ish sliding scale.
- **Good fit for:** Anyone who suspects they're bleeding money on forgotten subscriptions and wants a low-effort first step, not a full budgeting system.
- **Downside:** Thin as an actual budgeting tool. Don't expect YNAB- or Monarch-level planning depth.

## EveryDollar — best free zero-based option

EveryDollar (from the Ramsey ecosystem) is a simpler, more opinionated take on zero-based budgeting than YNAB — manual entry is free forever; automatic bank syncing is a paid add-on.

- **Cost:** Free for manual entry; paid tier for bank sync.
- **Good fit for:** Beginners who want to learn zero-based budgeting without a subscription, and don't mind entering transactions by hand.
- **Downside:** The free version's manual entry gets tedious fast if you have a lot of accounts or transaction volume.

## How to actually choose

Ask yourself one question first: **do you want to plan, or do you want to observe?** YNAB and EveryDollar are planning tools — you decide where money goes before it's spent. Monarch, Copilot, and Rocket Money are observation tools — they show you what already happened, cleanly, and let you react. Neither approach is wrong, but picking the wrong category for your actual habits is the most common reason people abandon a budgeting app within the first month.

If you're not sure which category fits, start with whichever has a real free tier — Rocket Money or EveryDollar's manual mode — before paying for a subscription you might not stick with.
