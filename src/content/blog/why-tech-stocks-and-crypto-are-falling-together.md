Tech stocks and crypto have both slid in the past week, and the stock move was bigger than "slight." On Thursday, November 5, 2026, the Nasdaq Composite closed about 5.5% lower, its worst day since April 2025, according to Fox Business. Bitcoin fell to a seven-week low the same day, and slipped again over the weekend to about $73,800, its lowest level since August, according to CoinDesk. By Monday, stocks were bouncing while crypto was still choppy.

This post is a snapshot **as of the afternoon of Monday, November 9, 2026 (UTC)**. It explains what is behind the moves, who is saying what, and what is still unclear. It makes no predictions about prices and recommends no stocks or coins.

## Who this is for, and who it isn't

**It's for:** anyone who owns a retirement account, index fund or crypto and saw alarming headlines, and anyone wondering why two very different assets seem to move together.

**It isn't** a guide to buying the dip or selling the rally. Nobody reliably times those, and this blog doesn't give personalized advice. If you want the basics first, see [stock market indices explained](/blog/stock-market-indices-explained-sp-500-dow-jones-and-nasdaq/) and [what is cryptocurrency](/blog/what-is-cryptocurrency-how-it-works-and-the-risks/).

## What happened, in order

All figures are as reported by the outlets named. Index moves are rounded.

- **Thursday, Nov 5.** The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5% and the S&P 500 about 4.4%, and the Dow lost roughly 1,800 points (about 3.4%), according to Fox Business. Fox said the Nasdaq was in correction territory, meaning at least 10% below its October 6 record close. Reports put the wipe-out in market value at roughly $2.3 to $2.5 trillion. Nvidia fell about 9%, the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell more than 12%, and data-center companies such as IREN, Applied Digital and Nebius each fell more than 14%.
- **Same day, crypto.** Bitcoin dropped below $76,000, a seven-week low, with reports of ETF outflows, according to Bloomberg's headline (I could not read the article).
- **Friday, Nov 6.** A partial rebound: the Nasdaq rose about 1.1% and chip stocks jumped, while the Dow slipped about 0.5%. The October jobs report showed 165,000 new jobs against an economist forecast of 65,000, with unemployment at 4.1%, per Fox Business.
- **Weekend, Nov 7-8.** Two headlines hit risk appetite. Nvidia's board said it opened an independent investigation after reports about CEO Jensen Huang, including whether he was targeted by a foreign intelligence service. Nvidia said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. The intelligence reports are unconfirmed. Separately, US-Iran talks stalled, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran's demands "outrageous."
- **Late Sunday, Nov 8.** Bitcoin touched about $73,761, then recovered above $75,000. Ether fell below $2,100 before bouncing to about $2,144. More than $1 billion of leveraged crypto positions were liquidated in 24 hours, according to CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk.
- **Monday, Nov 9.** TheStreet reported the Nasdaq and S&P 500 up, while Nvidia was down roughly 5%.

## Why the stock selloff happened

No single cause is established, and analysts disagree about how much each mattered. These are the explanations given.

**1. Worry about AI spending and politics.** Fox Business said investors were weighing what the Democrats' midterm wins could mean for AI regulation and data-center construction, with rising electricity prices making data centers a political issue. eToro analyst Jakub Rochlitz said markets were "pricing in the midterm results" and that the AI buildout could become slower and more expensive. This blog takes no side on the politics. The point is that markets were repricing a possible change in costs, not reacting to a party.

**2. Selling feeds on itself.** Argent Capital's Jed Ellerbroek told Reuters the move looked like "capitulation" among stocks tied to data-center spending, though he thought its size was outsized. A Bloomberg headline on November 6 said systematic funds, which trade by rules rather than judgment, sped up the drop. I could only read that headline.

**3. Interest rates are not helping.** The Federal Reserve held its rate at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28 in a 10-2 vote. The two dissenters wanted a *rise*, and the Fed said inflation "remains elevated" (federalreserve.gov). One market preview (BYDFi, November 4) said traders priced roughly a 73% to 77% chance of a rate rise at the December 8-9 meeting. Higher rates tend to hit fast-growing tech companies, whose profits are expected further in the future. For how this works, see [what the Fed does and how rate decisions affect your money](/blog/what-the-federal-reserve-does-and-how-rate-decisions-affect-your-money/).

## Why crypto moved with stocks

Bitcoin does not run on company profits, but it often trades like a "risk asset," meaning something investors sell when they get nervous. Several things pushed the same way this week:

- **The same nerves.** When big money cuts risk, it sells whatever it can sell quickly, and crypto trades around the clock, so it can feel the move first.
- **Leverage.** Many crypto traders borrow to bet on rising prices. When prices dip, exchanges force those positions closed, which pushes prices lower and forces more closures. CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk showed $913 million of the $1 billion-plus liquidated were bets on rising prices, with ether's liquidations ($448 million) almost double bitcoin's ($272 million).
- **Oil and geopolitics.** CoinDesk noted that bitcoin has often fallen during oil rallies since the Iran conflict began in March. That is the article's observation, not a proven rule.
- **Other crypto-specific news.** The [Binance hack](/blog/the-binance-hack-explained-what-happened-and-what-it-means-for-you/) was still fresh, and Fortune cited the midterm results as a factor for crypto too.

It was not a uniform drop. Fortune reported bitcoin down only about 1% over 24 hours, near $75,000, while ether fell nearly 5%, and XRP was down nearly 12% over the week. Ned Davis Research's John LaForge said bitcoin usually falls less than smaller coins when prices drop broadly. Bitwise's Ish Asad argued much of the bad news was "priced in." Those are opinions, not forecasts you can rely on. For scale, Fortune also noted bitcoin fell below $60,000 in June, so $75,000 is about 25% above that low (calculated for this post).

## A worked example: when two holdings fall together

This example is invented, with round numbers.

Say you have $10,000: $6,000 in a tech-heavy stock fund, $2,000 in crypto and $2,000 in a savings account. Suppose the tech fund falls 5.5% in a day and the crypto falls 5%:

- Tech fund: $6,000 × 5.5% = $330 lost, now $5,670
- Crypto: $2,000 × 5% = $100 lost, now $1,900
- Savings: unchanged at $2,000
- New total: $5,670 + $1,900 + $2,000 = **$9,570**, a 4.3% drop

The loss is smaller than either headline number because the savings account did not move. But the tech fund and crypto *did* fall together, so owning both spread the risk less than it looks. What protected this person was money placed where it was never exposed to either.

## What to check when markets fall

- **When do you need this money?** Money you need within a few years should not depend on stock or crypto prices, whatever this week's headlines say. See [how much you should have in an emergency fund](/blog/how-much-emergency-fund/).
- **How much of your savings is in one theme?** A fund tracking big tech, plus a crypto position, can be more of the same bet than it seems. [Stock market indices explained](/blog/stock-market-indices-explained-sp-500-dow-jones-and-nasdaq/) shows what the Nasdaq is made of.
- **Are you borrowing to invest?** Leverage is what turns dips into forced selling.
- **What is your plan?** For the general case on swings, see [what stock market volatility means for your savings](/blog/what-stock-market-volatility-means-for-your-savings/). For where bitcoin differs from other crypto, see [bitcoin vs ethereum vs stablecoins](/blog/bitcoin-vs-ethereum-vs-stablecoins-whats-the-difference/).

## What is still unknown

- **How much of the stock drop was politics, AI worries, the Fed or forced selling.** Different sources weight them differently.
- **What Nvidia's investigation will find,** and how much the unconfirmed reports matter to the company.
- **What inflation does next.** The US October consumer-price report is due Tuesday, November 10 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern, and it could move rate expectations, according to BYDFi's preview.
- **Whether the bounce lasts.** Stocks rose Friday and Monday, but nobody can say whether it holds.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating one day as a trend.** The Nasdaq's worst day in over a year was followed by gains, and one move says little about the next.
- **Assuming crypto protects you from stocks.** This week it didn't.
- **Reading a quote as a forecast.** "The bottom is in" and "support at $70,000" are opinions. Nobody knows.
- **Trading on rumors.** The intelligence reports about Nvidia are unconfirmed, and early reports in fast-moving stories often change. See [how to read crypto news without getting burned](/blog/how-to-read-crypto-news-without-getting-burned/).
- **Panic-selling money you won't need for years.** If your timeline hasn't changed, a headline hasn't changed it.

## Getting started

1. **List where your money sits:** stock funds, crypto, cash, and when you need each.
2. **Check for overlap:** are several holdings really the same theme?
3. **Check your emergency fund,** so a market drop doesn't force you to sell.
4. **Pick sources you can verify,** such as the Fed's own statements, and treat social media and price talk with caution.
5. **Write down any change you'd make** and sleep on it before acting.
