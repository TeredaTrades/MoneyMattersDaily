Moving your income and spending across borders adds a layer most budgeting advice doesn't cover. Here's what to get right first.

## Banking that travels with you

A home-country bank account tied to a single physical branch is the first thing to fix. Look for:

- A bank or fintech with low or zero foreign transaction fees
- Multi-currency accounts if you're paid in one currency and spending in another
- ATM fee reimbursement — this adds up fast over a year of travel

## Currency risk is a real budget line

If your income and expenses are in different currencies, exchange rate swings function like an invisible pay cut or raise. Two ways to manage it:

- Hold a buffer in your spending currency so short-term swings don't force bad-timed conversions
- Convert in batches rather than constantly, to average out volatility instead of chasing it

## Taxes don't pause because you're moving

Tax residency rules vary enormously by country and by how many days you spend where. The two things worth confirming before you leave, not after:

- Whether you're still a tax resident of your home country while abroad
- Whether the countries you're spending time in have their own thresholds that could make you liable there too

This is genuinely one area worth a real accountant, not a blog post — the cost of getting it wrong (double taxation, penalties) is much higher than the cost of an hour of professional advice.

## Getting started

Before your first trip: open a fee-free banking option, set a currency buffer, and confirm your home tax residency status. Everything else — travel insurance, remote-work visas, cost-of-living comparisons — matters, but those three get expensive fastest when ignored.
