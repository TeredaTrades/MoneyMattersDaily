Most "best banking app" comparisons are written for people who bank in one country and travel occasionally. Digital nomads have a different problem: money moving across currencies and borders constantly, ATM access in whatever country they're in this month, and no single home branch to fall back on. The five apps below solve different pieces of that problem — pricing and features are approximate and change, so confirm current terms before choosing.

## Wise — best for holding and converting multiple currencies

Wise (formerly TransferWise) built its entire product around currency conversion at close to the real mid-market rate, and its multi-currency account lets you hold balances in dozens of currencies at once with local account details (a US routing number, a UK sort code) in several of them.

- **Cost:** Free to hold; small percentage fee (often under 1%) to convert between currencies; a debit card with limited free ATM withdrawals per month before a per-withdrawal fee kicks in.
- **Good fit for:** Anyone regularly getting paid in one currency and spending in several others — freelancers invoicing international clients, remote employees paid abroad.
- **Downside:** It's not a full bank account with lending or credit products — it's a conversion and holding tool, so most nomads pair it with something else for savings or credit.

## Revolut — best for an all-in-one app with budgeting built in

Revolut covers similar multi-currency ground to Wise but wraps it in a broader app — budgeting, spare-change savings ("vaults"), stock and crypto trading, and travel insurance on higher tiers.

- **Cost:** Free tier available; paid tiers (roughly $10–$50/month depending on level) unlock higher free ATM limits, insurance, and airport lounge access.
- **Good fit for:** Someone who wants one app handling banking, budgeting, and some investing, rather than stitching several apps together.
- **Downside:** Free-tier ATM withdrawal limits are low, and the free tier's currency conversion has weekend/volume markups that the paid tiers remove — casual users often end up needing a paid plan to get the experience they expected from the free one.

## N26 — best if you're based long-term in the EU

N26 is a German-licensed digital bank offering a full EU IBAN account, which matters for anyone who needs to receive rent, salary, or local payments the way an EU resident normally would, not just hold foreign currency as a visitor.

- **Cost:** Free tier; paid tiers (roughly €5–€17/month) add multi-currency sub-accounts, more free ATM withdrawals, and insurance.
- **Good fit for:** A nomad based in or frequently returning to the EU who needs a genuine local account, not just a travel card.
- **Downside:** Account opening requires EU residency in practice for most applicants, so it doesn't work as a first account for someone who hasn't spent meaningful time there yet.

## Charles Schwab Investor Checking — best for fee-free ATM withdrawals worldwide

This isn't a nomad-specific app, but it's a recurring recommendation in nomad communities for one specific reason: Schwab refunds all ATM fees worldwide, including the fee the ATM operator itself charges, with no foreign transaction fee on top.

- **Cost:** Free, no minimum balance, no monthly fee — must be opened alongside a linked Schwab brokerage account, which itself has no minimum.
- **Good fit for:** A US-based nomad who wants to keep withdrawing local cash without paying ATM fees anywhere, without needing to route money through a fintech first.
- **Downside:** US residents only in practice for opening the account, and it's a checking account with no built-in multi-currency holding — you're withdrawing local cash on demand rather than holding balances in multiple currencies.

## Chime — best simple option for domestic-first US freelancers going abroad occasionally

Chime is a US-only mobile banking app with no foreign transaction fees on card purchases, though it doesn't refund ATM operator fees the way Schwab does.

- **Cost:** Free, no monthly fees, no minimum balance.
- **Good fit for:** A US-based freelancer who travels internationally sometimes but isn't a full-time nomad, and wants a simple no-fee card for purchases abroad without setting up a multi-currency account.
- **Downside:** ATM withdrawals abroad still incur the ATM operator's own fee (Chime doesn't waive it), and there's no multi-currency holding — it solves card purchases abroad, not the broader nomad banking problem.

## How to actually choose

The honest answer for most full-time nomads is two accounts, not one: **a multi-currency holding/conversion tool (Wise or Revolut) for getting paid and converting between currencies, plus a fee-free ATM card (Schwab, if you're US-based) for pulling local cash without losing money to withdrawal fees.** N26 is the exception — worth it on its own if you're genuinely EU-based rather than just passing through. Chime fits a narrower case: occasional international travel layered on an otherwise domestic-first setup, not full-time nomad banking.

Whatever you pick, test it with a small transfer and a small ATM withdrawal before relying on it for real income — fee structures and ATM networks both vary more in practice than any comparison table fully captures.
